## Robot and world
- Three vehicles, one row in Vehicle (Unicycle · Car (kinematic) · Car (dynamic: tyre slip)):
  - Unicycle (differential drive): exact arc integration, robot radius 0.3 m, can turn in place.
  - Car-like (kinematic bicycle), pose at the rear-axle centre: θ̇ = v tan δ / L. Defaults: L = 0.6 m, δ_max = 32° (R_min 0.96 m), steering rate 1.5 rad/s, body 0.9 × 0.5 m. Physics uses the exact oriented rectangle; planners, DWA and MPC use 3 covering circles (≈0.14 m conservative at the bumpers).
  - Car with tyre slip (dynamic bicycle model): the same body and pose, plus lateral velocity v_y and yaw rate r at the centre of mass; linear tyres clipped at μF_z. Presets Grippy and Slippery floor. See "Car with tyre slip" below.
- Actuation: control latency (whole 50 ms ticks, command queue) → saturation → first-order lag τ → acceleration limits → (car) steering-rate limit → actuator bias (car steering misalignment in degrees; diff turn-rate bias in rad/s).
- Sensors: wheel odometry reporting (v, ω) (noise + calibration bias the filters are not told about), 120-beam 360° lidar (8 m, 10 Hz), range/bearing beacons (5 Hz, line-of-sight, optional unlabeled + clutter), an optional yaw-rate gyro, and an optional forward radar (range, bearing, radial velocity; see "Radar and weather" below). Weather (clear, rain, fog) degrades the lidar.
- Beacon availability (Localize tab): switch single beacons off (numbered chips, or Tap map → Switch a beacon on/off; `bOff`, comma-separated IDs) and random outages (`bOut` % of the time down, mean length `bOutLen` s; short = flicker). Each beacon is a two-state Markov chain stepped per beacon scan, on its own random stream (`S.rngB`), so the sensor noise sequence is unchanged. Map: switched off = grey hollow diamond with a stroke, outage = hollow. Dead reckoning draws no beacon readings (it never used them; the rays made it look connected), and its Localize tab says so, with a one-click "add odometry noise" when the level's odometry is perfect (L1, L2: DR = truth there). L3 Try: EKF with the east beacons off (error RMS 0.25 m vs 0.05 m, 90 s), flickering (50 %, 0.4 s: 0.08 m), long outages (70 %, 6 s: 0.14 m); all twelve off gives 1.51 m, like dead reckoning.
- The world is the level's size: 24 × 16 m for the built-in levels, up to 48 × 32 m (at most 1 536 m²) for a custom or generated one (Levels as data, below); 0.1 m grids, 20 Hz fixed step.

## Subsystems implemented
1. Localization: ground truth, dead reckoning, EKF (Joseph form, Mahalanobis gating), particle filter (low-variance resampling, augmented MCL + sensor-resetting injection, lidar likelihood field, motion-gated updates). NEES consistency metric.
2. Perception: prior map or log-odds occupancy mapping in the estimated frame; distance-transform costmap with inflation. Moving agents (v7): ground truth or a lidar tracker (unexplained hits → beam clusters → constant-velocity Kalman filter per agent, χ² gate 9.21, greedy nearest neighbour, confirm after 3 hits, coast 1 s); prediction constant velocity or frozen, radius r + g·t.
3. High-level planning: mission FSM (IDLE/PLAN/DRIVE/NO_PATH/LOST/DONE), replan on blocked / off path (estimate > 1 m from the path, e.g. after a kidnap) / stuck / timer, lost-guard, waypoint ordering (in order, greedy, exact TSP). Multi-leg plans with cusps; posed goals for the car.
4. Motion planning: straight line, A*, RRT*; car only: Reeds–Shepp (endpoint-verified) and Hybrid A* (RS analytic expansion, max(2D Dijkstra, RS) heuristic, planning at 85% of δ_max). Start leniency: a car whose start reads below the 0.15 m margin may lose one 0.1 m grid cell of clearance within 1 m of the start. A start within one cell above the margin gets the same leniency only when the strict search runs out of nodes (2026-10-11: such poses in the L6 parallel slot found no way out, because a 1 cm move drops the quantized reading by a cell).
5. Control: pure pursuit, rear-wheel feedback, Stanley (car), PID, DWA, MPC, user JavaScript controller in a Web Worker.

## MPC
- One solver, two formulations: NMPC (relinearize about the latest nonlinear rollout every iteration) and LTV-MPC (linearize once about the reference, predict with the linear model, convex QP).
- Model: x = [x, y, θ, v, s] with s = ω (diff) or δ (car); u = [a, ṡ], so acceleration and steering-rate / angular-acceleration limits are exact box constraints. Optional actuator-lag model adds [v_cmd, s_cmd] (7 states). Steering angle / ω saturates inside the model.
- Command in Δu form (v6): command = own previous command + u̇·T, with bumpless transfer (falls back to the measured state when the MPC did not run on the previous tick, e.g. on PLAN ticks). This gives the MPC integral action on its input, so it rejects a constant diff-drive turn-rate bias. It does not see car steering misalignment (bias added after the steering state).
- Cost: along-track, lateral, heading and speed error against a time-parametrized reference (same speed profile as the other trackers), terminal multiplier, input weights on a and ṡ, Δu weight between moves.
- Horizons: prediction horizon Np and control horizon Nc (move blocking: u_k = u_{Nc−1} for k ≥ Nc; 2·Nc decision variables). Step Δt tunable.
- Constraints: input box (exact, projected-Newton box QP); speed sign/limit per leg, |s| ≤ s_max and lidar obstacle clearance (footprint circles ≥ margin, NMPC only) via augmented Lagrangian with tunable ρ.
- Solver: single-shooting Gauss–Newton SQP with forward sensitivities and central-difference Jacobians (forward differences read zero steering sensitivity at full left lock, where the steering state saturates, so the car stalled at cusps that end on +δ_max), backtracking line search, warm start shifted by one tick (RTI-style), 15 ms budget. Typical solve ≈ 1 ms, worst observed ≈ 15 ms.
- Results (headless runs): L1 cross-track RMS 0.051 m (rear-wheel feedback 0.083, pure pursuit 0.105, LTV 0.089, Nc = 1 0.178). L5 with τ = 0.15 s: 0.082 m with and without the lag model after the Δu change (the lag model changes the loop margins more than the tracking error). L4: NMPC with obstacle constraints 4/4; LTV clips the table. L6: NMPC parks 4/4 with no collisions; LTV collides in the parallel slot.
- Known limits: obstacle constraints use a lidar snapshot (people treated as static); the reference is per leg, so the MPC does not plan through a cusp. A minimum-progress constraint was tried for L6 and made things worse (circling); reverted.

## Ultrasonic ring (2026-10-20; branch `claude/sonar-ring`)
Owner request: more sensor models, each taught by its failure modes; this one runs in parallel with a radar and a GNSS session, which builds the Sensors panel. The ring is one boolean (`P.sonar`, off by default) and its parameters, so the panel can switch it; `core/sensors.js` has the registry entry it should get as a comment, since `SENSORS` is not on this branch.
- **Sensor** (`sonarSense`, `sonEcho`, `sonRay` in `core/sensors.js`): `sonN` sonars (8; 4–16) evenly round the hull (the circle of radius `RR`, the car's rectangle), each a cone `sonBeam` wide (30°) cast as a fan of rays 2° apart from the true pose; a ray's echo counts if it is beyond the dead zone `sonMin` (0.2 m), within `sonMax` (4 m) and met within `sonSpec` (30°) of square-on (`sonRay` returns the incidence cosine: the slab face hit, the circle's normal); the reading is the nearest counting echo plus `sonSig` noise (1 cm), else the maximum (`why`: ok, spec, dead, none, xt). Crosstalk: with probability `sonXt` (2 %) a reading is a neighbour's echo (phenomenological). The ring fires in index order, one ping per `sonPing` (50 ms), so the cycle is 0.4 s. Its own RNG stream (seed · 6151 + 5), created at the first ping: with the ring off nothing runs and the L1–L5 summary and the Node golden file are unchanged. Readings go through the sensor-latency queue like the lidar's (`sensEnqueue('sonar')`), are placed with the estimate on delivery (`sonarApply`, `S.sonArcs`, a new array per delivery so replay frames keep theirs) and drawn by the Sonar ring layer.
- **Mapping** (`integrateSonar` in `core/perception.js`, Perceive → What builds the map: lidar, sonar ring, both; `P.mapSrc`): the arc inverse sensor model of chapter 3 §11. Arc cells (centres within 0.07 m of the range, inside the beam) that the map does not already know free (`L_KNOWN` −0.5) share $l = \ln((n+1)/(n-1))$, capped at `L_OCC`; the cone before the arc gets `L_SFREE` −0.2; a maximum-range reading marks nothing. Counting every arc cell instead (uniform) costs a goal and doubles the collisions on the rough-wall run.
- **Safety stop** (`guardPts` in `core/dispatch.js`, Control → The safety stop reads; `P.guardSrc`): each sonar's latest arc (up to two cycles old) as points every 3°, the whole arc since the bearing is unknown, alone or added to the lidar's hits.
- **Particle filter** (`pfSonarUpdate`, `sonExpect` in `core/localization.js`, Localize → Also weight particles by the sonar ring; `P.pfSonar`, with the prior map): the expected reading is the nearest map obstacle in the cone (7 rays 5° apart, sphere-traced on the distance field, beyond the dead zone); Gaussian σ 0.15 m with a 0.05 floor for returned readings, 0.9 / 0.3 for a maximum reading with nothing / something in reach (0.3: about a third of the ring's readings in the warehouse are specular misses); tempered by 0.5, not while standing still (`f.mS`). σ 0.25 was worse (RMS 0.18 m against 0.13).
- **Code tab**: the localizer gets `obs.sonar` (the readings delivered this tick, body frame), the predictor `obs.sonar` (each sonar's latest arc on the map and its age).
- **Measured** (Perceive lesson 9, chapter 3 §11; seed 7, checked at 3 and 11; `tests/measure_per.py` and `tests/measure_loc.py`, which now take `seed` and a Try's `goals`, and report floor seen, contacts made standing still, seconds the safety stop capped the speed and how the ring's readings came back): sonar-only mapping fails the mission in the unknown building and the aisles (0 and 1 goals of 4, the first collision with a wall met at a slant); with rougher walls (`sonSpec` 50°) the map is the arc model's blur (precision 0.88, recall 0.75 against 1.00 / 0.96) and the mission completes at one seed of three; the cautious policy (unknown is lethal) never moves, the cones having seen too little floor. The sonar safety stop misses both walkers who react on L5's cross aisle (the lidar's misses none) because of the dead zone: the miss replaces the echo; with `sonMin` 0 it holds. The sonar-only particle filter localizes on L3 (5 of 5 goals, RMS 0.07–0.13 m against the lidar's 0.06), overconfident (inside 2σ 71–98 %), and is indifferent to 30 % crosstalk.
- **Verification**: `tests/node/sonar.test.mjs` (off leaves no state; a run is repeatable; the sonar-only filter localizes); the lesson's Try buttons in `p5test.py`, the chapter's live values and links in `chtest.py`, the terms in `tles.py`.
- **Known limits and next steps**: no materials (glass, dark or shiny surfaces), so the sonar's real advantage over a lidar never shows; a lidar that misses glass would be the honest pair for it (a material per shape in the level format). No hold of the last close echo, so the dead zone shows; real rings keep it for a cycle or two. The expectation of the cone likelihood ignores the specular rule (the grid has no normals; the distance field's gradient could give them). Firing is in index order, not interleaved (real rings fire opposite sonars together and skip neighbours to cut crosstalk), and crosstalk is a probability, not a sound field. The Map source still says Lidar mapping when the sonar builds the map. Corner reflections (an inside corner returns a strong echo) and multipath long readings are not modelled.

- **Rates** (`riskRate` in `core/search.js`, shared by Tools → Safety and `tools/search.mjs`): the random half of a search as hours of operation (each run's `opS`), events per hour for moving contacts with a person, strikes, or any contact; exact Garwood interval by bisection on the Poisson CDF (`poissonCI`); `riskHoursFor` is the one-sided rule of three, −ln 0.05 / target. The refined half is left out (chosen for being bad).
- **Importance sampling** (`searchSample(sh, u, imp)`): people drawn from q(x) = 2x over their range, weight 1 / (2x) on the continuous draw (unbiased for anything the run depends on although the generator rounds the count), x clipped at 0.05 (weights ≤ 10); the plain draws are unchanged (`risk.test.mjs`). Weighted rate Σwk / ΣwT with a seeded bootstrap interval (1 000 resamples); with no event, the rule of three on Kish effective hours (ΣwT)² / Σw²T.
- **Leading indicators** (`spiTable`): Spearman's ρ of each per-hour measure (seconds under 0.3 m, seconds with TTC under 1 s, seconds with a person within 2 m, closest approach, safety-stop seconds, brake stops) against contacts per hour across the random runs; the best charted run by run.
- **Page**: an importance-sampling option and `cfg.saf.imp`; the Risk table with the hours a 1-per-1000-h claim needs; the indicators' table and chart. The page and Node give the same rates for the same search seed (checked on lesson 8's second Try).
- **Findings** (40 random runs per search): ODD-1 has 9.7–14.6 contacts with a person per hour (8.4–22.5 at 95 %) and 7–9.5 strikes; with people who react, no strike in 1.24–1.34 h on three search seeds (below 2.8–3.0 per hour) and no moving contact on two; importance sampling gives the same ODD-1 rates within their intervals, and with reacting people (no strike either way) a wider bound on every seed (3.75–4.86 against 2.76–2.97), the weights spending effective hours; in every search with contacts a closeness measure ranks first and time within 2 m last.
- **Verification**: `tests/node/risk.test.mjs`, `saftest.py` (the Risk table).

## Moving agents (v7)
- Before v7 people only reached the stack as lidar hits: the global planner never saw them, DWA and MPC treated hits as static at scan time, and pure pursuit ignored them.
- Architecture: global path static on purpose; agents handled at the trajectory level (path–velocity decomposition); escalate to a replan only when speed alone cannot resolve the conflict.
- Path trackers (pure pursuit, rear-wheel feedback, Stanley, PID): Moving agents = Ignore / Reactive stop (brake for unexplained hits in the footprint corridor) / Speed planning. The ST planner runs DP over (t, s, v) with Δt 0.2 s and horizon T_p. Costs: speed error to v_ref(s), acceleration, proximity, a hard cost when the predicted bodies overlap (soft cost in the uncertainty band), and a terminal cost so the profile cannot end parked in an agent's lane just past the horizon. The speed at 0.2 s caps the tracker and curvature is kept.
- Escalation: unavoidable conflict for about 0.3 s, or about 4 s of waiting, triggers a replan. That plan alone has agents' predicted positions over the next 2 s in the costmap.
- DWA checks each rollout against the agents' predicted positions at the same time step. NMPC gets one clearance constraint per footprint circle and step against the nearest predicted agent; the last step must also stay clear while the robot would wait there, out to T_p + 3 s (the speed planner's terminal test), and each agent constraint is relaxed by what braking to a stop now would violate, so it stays feasible when a person walks into the robot. With Speed planning the MPC's reference follows the ST profile, and the speed planner's escalation (replan on an agent blocking) applies to it as to the path trackers. Hits from tracked agents are removed from the static point set.
- UI: ST graph telemetry tab (occupancy, chosen profile, the profile without agents, MPC/DWA prediction projected on the path). Map layer with tracks, predicted rings at 1 s intervals, and ego predicted positions with matching time numbers. New metrics: contacts into the stopped robot, closest approach, track counts. Symbols group, notes, and obs.agents for custom code.
- L6 now has 4 pedestrians crossing the lanes, pausing 1–3 s at the ends of their walks (this breaks constant-velocity prediction when they stop and when they set off again).
- Results (people contacts per mission):
  - L5 seed 7: Ignore 3, Reactive 3, ST with lidar tracking 1, ST with ground truth 0.
  - L5 seeds 2–5: ST with lidar ≈1 per run versus 3 when ignoring. The residual case is a person reversing at the end of their walk beside the robot.

  - L5 MPC with predictions 0 versus 3 without.
  - L6 MPC (seeds 7, 1–4): before 4 contacts in 5 runs, closest −0.30…0.16 m; after 0 contacts, closest 0.40–0.57 m. ## Loop analysis (v6)
- Simulation/Analysis switch above the map. Analysis covers the lateral tracking loop for pure pursuit, rear-wheel feedback, Stanley, PID and MPC (DWA and user code are not linearizable here).
- Linearization: straight path at v₀ = min(cruise, v_max). Plant θ̇ₑ = g·u (g = 1 diff, v₀/L car), ė = v₀·θₑ. Controller gains are read from the actual controller code by central finite differences on a sandboxed copy of the simulator state (MPC also gives gains on the steering state, the lagged command and its own previous rate).
- L(jω): continuous vehicle behind a zero-order hold (e^{−sT/2}) plus latency; discrete elements (actuator lag, PID integral/derivative, MPC rate integrator and memory) are evaluated exactly at z = e^{sT}.
- Exact 20 Hz linear closed loop gives the step and bias responses and the closed-loop poles (s = ln z / T); root locus scales the controller ×0.06–×16.
- Outputs: block diagram; chips for stability, PM, GM (negative when unstable), ω_c, delay margin PM/ω_c, M_s, dominant ζ/ω_n, overshoot, settling, steady error under actuator bias; Bode (optional |S|, |T|), Nyquist (compressed radius log₂(1+|L|) or linear, closest approach 1/M_s, encirclement count Z = N + P), time response, pole/zero + locus.
- Experiments in the real nonlinear simulator: step test (overshoot, settling, time on the steering-rate limit) and sine injection at the plant input (L = −U_c/U_p by lock-in, small or large amplitude) to compare against the model.
- Verification: injection matches the model within 0.1 dB / 1° for PP, RWF, PID, MPC (with/without lag and lag model, car, latency); predicted and simulated step metrics agree; Nyquist count and eigenvalues agree in every lesson configuration.
- Learn tab: 10 lessons with Try buttons — the loop, pure pursuit as PD, Bode, Nyquist, delay margin and limit cycles, poles and damping (RWF ζ = k_θ/(2√k_e)), actuator lag and what MPC assumes, integral action, linear vs nonlinear, gain scheduling with speed.
